Blue Landscape
# Beginnings It was December 24th, 1980. A young woman very pregnant was ready to give birth. The night snow moved in, but the baby lingered as the storm grew with her contractions. Early the next morning, before the sun rose, Christmas day, a holiday storm raged. The young woman knew the baby was ready. Off to the hospital she went through rising snowdrifts. The storm whipped and blew during the long, treacherous drive, but they arrived safely. She eased out of the car, holding her belly while trying to shield her eyes from the wind and snow. Her steps were careful and her breathing light and accelerated. Nurses quickly admitted her to a room. Within hours she had a new baby boy in her arms. But the wind was still howling. She wrapped the cloak she had arrived in around her shoulders, tightly wrapping her and her new baby. She got back to the house, baby bundled and hidden. Carrying the hidden baby, she just looked, still pregnant. So on Christmas morning her parents and siblings were there as she slowly walked in, snow falling from her head and shoulders onto the floor. She pulled back her cloak hood revealing a sweet smile. From under the cloak she presented her parents with a gift, a new baby boy. # The Cage I was that baby. Bright eyed, gentle, restless, unafraid. But I had a sleeping problem. Staying awake, sometimes for days at a time. Old pictures show a little blond boy with dark, heavy under-eye bags. So the little climbing boy who couldn't sleep and liked to climb and then wasn't afraid to jump got a lid to his crib. My grandfather, a carpenter, repurposed a side from another crib to fashion a top for my crib. So I lay awake in a cage... I remember a creeping madness, being stuck alone for many dark hours. But I found a way out. A jump-off point where I would leap off towards a thought, a mental image. And I would follow it and get caught in a current of imagination. Like flying, a story would begin and I would start flowing through it. This saved me. I pressed through the canopy, avoiding every branch and leaf, smooth and fast, like flying Then I am me again, some circumstance unfolds, I play it out, reliving the story or just imagining a different time-line. I could think of anything, unfold anything. The feeling, a freedom that feels both expansive and engulfing. Those imagined flights carried me through the long nights when I was small. I learned to slip the bars of my crib, not with my body but with my mind. For years, that was enough—to escape into stories, to drift through worlds no one else could see. But in time, real awakenings came too. Moments that felt just as dreamlike, but belonged to the waking world. # Conflict I hear the loudest crashing, and yelling. My open eyes see darkness. I stumble out of bed to the stairwell. Dylan is halfway down the stairs crouching holding a baseball bat. The crashing the smashing the yelling are all louder now. The crashing continues and morphs into screaming and crying. My father is attacking my mother and I am frozen, there beside my brother. Between us you can feel fear and maybe shame. Like watching some terrible accident that you can do nothing about. We aren't crying, we are in shock. I hear other voices enter. The loud voices escalate then stop as doors close and silence now. I return to bed but not to sleep, I never sleep, especially not tonight... # Pieces of Home Awakening I blink, rubbing my eyes. Upon sitting up I notice a note on the bedstand. “Meet me at the swamp at sunrise - Dylan”. I smile and start getting dressed. Light peeks out at me from the Eastern horizon. I hurry to not be late. Leaving the house I cross the road and start walking down the trail towards the swamp. As I climb the last rise, acres of march stretch out before me. I take a hard right into the woods, I know where he is. In a section of swamp where it has crept into the forest. Many trees had fallen crisscrossing out onto the swamp. There he is, balanced on a downed tree in the middle of the swamp. I trace a path towards him, balancing on the crisscrossed downed trees. We sit there over the water, as the sun clears the tree tops. Breathing deep I take it all in. The smell of tadpoles and decaying peat. A gentle breeze. The warmth of the sun on my face and arms. ** I am drawn to one book, ‘The Second Ring of Power’ by Carlos Castaneda. I slide on my socks to a stop, slippery on the wooden floor. Picking up the book, I take in the woman and dog on the cover. I feel a touch of trepidation ripple through me. I open the book and start to read. But I just can’t follow. I can’t comprehend. It feels magic, like secrets that only I don’t know. I see the unfathomable mystery but I recognize it as a failing on my part. # Freedom Did you ever release yourself to the current? There is a simple flow to what is. No reasoning or change alters it. You cannot control it, nor need you. To try is only to create resistance. Do not add conflict to what is already perfect. Once you realize this, you see you were always part of it. You are the current. Water does not try to swim. # Karma The water you swim in is karma: every interaction you have had, every interaction you will have, perhaps even every life you have lived or will live. For now, your concern is only the span from birth to this moment. The story you tell yourself must be cultivated. From there, you can choose with consciousness. # What is "it" It is a strata. A multi-layered, fractalated, spiraling field. It is a plein and a way. One of the things that exist in this flow or current is emotion. Another is time. And another is matter in a non physical state. # Chapter 1 John glanced both ways and crossed the street. The haberdashery windows threw sunlight into his eyes. Squinting, he gripped the tarnished brass doorknob and turned it hard. “John, good to see you!” Roland’s voice called from inside as John’s eyes adjusted to the dim interior. “Hello, Roland,” John replied. He paused, breathing in cedar and cologne, then slipped off his coat. Roland stepped forward, hand outstretched, and pulled him into a half hug. They separated, smiling. “How’s Janet?” Roland asked, his eyes warm with concern. “The doctors are doing their jobs. She’s not in pain, but no miracles yet.” John’s gaze drifted. “Still work to be done,” Roland said. He placed a firm hand on John’s shoulder. John rolled up his sleeves and nodded. Roland folded back half the carpet, revealing a hatch. John pulled it open; a damp, musky smell rose from below. He climbed down the ladder. Roland picked up a book from the counter and followed. In the basement, a circle of stones marked the earthen floor. John lay in the shallow impression at its center, where he had lain many times before. Roland sat outside the circle, opened the book, and read aloud. A shiver ran through John as the ground pulsed. His body lifted slightly. He inhaled and held his breath. The stones vanished in a flash of electric blue, and John fell into the void. Roland sat upright, closed the book, and exhaled. # Chapter 2 The blue glow rose around him, and he fell into it. The hole to the haberdashery basement shrank above as he dropped. Images formed in the light—memories of things John had seen, heard, or smelled—but they rushed past too quickly to grasp. His speed increased. Emotion surged within him, then everything went dark. No movement, no senses, no John. Only void. Time stretched, weightless, until the void shattered into infinite fractals. He was no longer John but a drop in an ocean, seeing and being all he ever was or would be. Another drop drifted with him—fractured, wounded, dying. Its facets pressed against his, manipulating timelines, nudging events, straining to heal the whole by mending fragments. John searched for a keystone, a pillar that could turn the tide toward life. # Chapter 3 John woke on the cool dirt floor. The dream had felt like lifetimes, yet here he was again, unchanged. As always, the other realm dissolved the moment he opened his eyes. He gathered himself, disheartened. Maybe this time, when he got home, things would be different. # Chapter 4 # Visiting a Friend Chapter 1 - Visiting a Freind The air smelled of damp earth and trodden grass. Magdaline’s red cape billowed in the cool breeze. Thinking of Timothy she smiled and picked up her pace. The letter in her pocket felt warm, like a piece of him. “Not far to go,” Magdaline said to herself, cresting a rise. In the distance she could see Timothy’s cottage. Her smile widened and she let out a cheer, resisting an urge to run down the road to the cottage. She settled into a brisk walk. Approaching the tiny cottage, she called out, “Timothy, you around?” The house was small, no more than a single room. The earthy red shingled roof matched the tan plaster outer walls. A door sat between two wooden framed windows. She knocked three times. “Hello?” Silence. She twisted the knob. Locked. She felt a stir of unease in her chest. Looking under a flat rock by the door she found a key. “Yoink.” She paused, went to the door, inserted the key. It turned and the lock clicked, the door opened. Inside, the "cottage" charm faded. The single room felt like a shack with wooden pine board floor, soft blue pine board walls, and rough-hewn furniture. On a countertop a folded note sat beside a red crystal that seemed to pulse a faint glow when she first noticed it. Examining the note, it was a list of supplies addressed to someone named Vox. She returned the list, glancing again at the crystal. Stepping out she locked the door and returned the key under the rock, pressing the stone into place. The town wasn’t far down the road. She started to walk as dusk set in. The forest grew darker. A warm breeze was rich with the scent of wild flowers and sweet grass. Leaving the forest and entering a clearing the day brightened again. A figure crouched in the road ahead. He neither looked up nor moved as she approached. “Looking for something?” he asked. “Someone, actually… a robot… I mean… my friend, Timothy.” Magdaline’s voice faltered; unsure of what to make of him. “Robots don’t last long out here,” the traveler said. “The Wild swallows them whole.” He never raised his head, his tone as heavy as the dusk around them. A shudder rippled through Magdaline, “I’ll be on my way”. She hurried on, relieved to leave him behind. Soon, the town opened up before her. The setting sun turned the sky to gold. She passed a giant oak tree at the edge of town where the air shifted, the fragrant flowers from the shopfronts overtaking the scents of the forest. The town was just a main street with houses and shop fronts lining the road. She walked into the first shop she came upon. A strange looking character sat behind a long countertop. Behind him on a shelf a peculiar looking fluffy cat sat curled up sleeping. “Hello, are you Vox?” said Magdaline, her voice steadier than she felt. “Ah, there you are. I was starting to wonder what path you’d take,” replied Vox, straightening. Two jet-black horns protruded from the top of his head. “You knew I was coming?” Magdaline stepped back, suspicion on her face. “I know someone always is. But who will it be? Who knows?” Behind him, the cat purred as if amused by his answer. "There was a man in the forest wearing a cloak, he was acting strange" she piped in. Vox leaned towards her. "The traveler. Sullen looking fellow, isn't he? He wonders through many stories, some of which were never meant to end". "I didn't trust him" said Magdaline, shuffleing her feet uncomfortably. "Good, that means your instincts are still working. Be careful where you follow him. Not all roads want to be walked". "I'm looking for my friend Timothy". She stated, tiring of the riddles. "Ah, yes, the gardener, quiet soul. Always dreaming of green". "Do you know where he went?" still looking for a straight answer. "He was chasing something... old," Said Vox "Why are you speaking so cryptically?" Magdaline's tone growing serious. "There's a place where the ground remembers. Where the moss hums and the stone breathes in its sleep". replied Vox, ignoring her plea. "In a cave? Underground?" Agitation stirred through Magdaline in response to another vague, confusing reply from Vox. "Perhaps. Or a memory shaped like one". "Do you think he's in danger?" Magdaline pressed. "He went to plant something. Could be in danger, I guess. East over the old bridge should get you where you're going". The cat purred loud, again. # A Shift in Perspective Chapter 2 - A Shift in Perspective Timothy burst into the clearing. Feeling something was internally wrong but he didn't know what. Fortune must have been smiling down: there ahead was his friend Fizzwick; local handyman, engineer, and friend. Timothy knew whatever was wrong with him he needed a friend and Fizzwick may know how to diagnose him. They walked towards each other, Fizzwick wrapped an arm around Timothy. "Ay, friend, you look like you've fallen on hard times!" said Fizzwick. A smile on his face and a glint in his eye. Timothy's servos jerked as he moved and sparks flew from somewhere, "I think my systems are failing," reflecting the warmth of Fizzwick's greeting, Timothy responded with humour, "and YOU look like YOU'VE fallen on hard times" "I HAVE FALLEN ON HARD TIMES!" Fizzwick wailed, dramatically, opening his mouth wide, revealing a missing front tooth. "Seriously though, if something's wrong, lets get you back to town, see if we can fix you up!" "Please and thank you", was all Timothy got out before a shower of sparks rained out from under his yellow cloak. He emitted a loud 'BANG', stiffened and fell limp to the ground. "TIMOTHY!" Fizzwick shouted to his unresponsive friend. He looked around fighting back a hopeless feeling welling in his chest. "Don't worry buddy, I'll get you outta here." # Sullen Lament Chapter 3 - Sullen Lament The next morning Magdaline set out again. Leaving town, dark clouds swirled in the sky, feeling the cool breeze she wrapped her cloak tighter. She crossed the large bridge that Vox spoke of. Following the road she rose in elevation. climbing raising foothills as she went. She could tell by the way the trees cleared ahead that she was nearing an overlook. She climbed until the top plateaued out before her. Ahead lay an outcropping of rock, forming a cave entrance. And there beside the mouth of the caves stood the Sullen Traveler. Magdaline was startled to see him again, how did he get here. He couldn't have followed her, he got here first. Smirking, he was holding a red crystal, just like the one she had seen on Timothy's counter. Magdaline strode up to him defiantly, "Where did you get that?" Motioning to the crystal he held. "Down there," The traveler stepped towards Magdaline, she flinched but held her ground. He nodded towards the cave and said "Why don't you see for yourself!" He shoved Magdaline hard, sending her plunging into the darkness of the cave below. Luckily her landing was softened by the thick moss layering the cave floor. The nearly vertical cave walls looked impossible to climb to get back out. She yelled "HEY," towards the light of the cave entrance far above. But she heard no response. Looking around her eyes began to adjust to the darkness. The only way was further into the cave. She stepped forward , the soft moss compressing under each step. A cold wind blew from further in where random drops of water could be heard. It smelled of wet stone and damp moss. She moved forward in chilly darkness. Ahead a faint red glow came from the ground. She walked up and there was what looked like a natural growth of the same kind of red glowing crystal. Magdaline stopped to examine it. "Timothy, what are you up to", she said aloud, concern in her voice. She looked down at he crystal a moment longer before continuing on. As she continued into the cave she could hear a faint hum. She passed other colored crystals that seemd to be emmiting light, one blue and one purple. She also passed some kind machine or control panel. It had strange writing on it she couldn't understand. In the dim cave light it looked tarnished and rusted. Then the cave opened up into a huge cavern. As her eyes adjusted a robot came into view. It was huge, a titan. She inhaled deeply trying to center her breath. But she walked towards it. The titan was sitting down, moss covered and overgrown with roots. It looked like it hadn't moved in years. As she neared it's outstretched feet it whirred to life, it's eyes blinking open. Magdaline stood frozen as they stared at each other, a young woman and a robot cave titan. "Ah, another wanderer", said the titan, "or perhaps you're something more" "What are you?" Magdaline asked through quick, furtive breaths. "I am keeper of secrets, roots grown wise." The titan paused "but you're not here for me, are you?" "Do you know where my friend Timothy is?" Magdaline blurted out, breathing fast and heavy, in the cool damp air.
