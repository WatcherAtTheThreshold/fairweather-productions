About Fairweather

Two Souls, One Sacred Vision

We are Shannon and Jessop Hunt, two artists who chose to dream together. Fairweather Studios is where our individual voices harmonize into something greater, creating a sanctuary where impressionistic wonder meets mystical visions, where each brushstroke carries both personal truth and universal magic.

Jessop Hunt

Seeker of Light and Shadow

Jessop works across ink, paint, sound, and story; moving in a recursive rhythm where a painting might inspire a song, a song a game, a game a story. One thing leads to the next, which leads to the next, ad infinitum. Beneath it all runs a current of spirituality and psychology: an eye for the god of small things, a question forever turned toward the why of self, the why of others.

Shannon Hunt

Guardian of the Sacred Feminine

Shannon channels ancient wisdom through contemporary vision, painting portals between worlds where goddesses whisper and spirits dance. Her acrylic paintings pulse with mythic energy, each piece a doorway to understanding the divine feminine that flows through all creation, weaving stories of transformation and empowerment.

A Bit About Us

Today, the creative legacy continues as we paint here as Fairweather Productions. After years crafting with our hands, facing the challenges of small-town business life, we turned fully to what called us both from the start: painting. Her luminous, elevated acrylics and my impressionistic oils, inks, and vectors come together in works rooted in myth, nature, and care. We've found our rhythm in galleries and shows, where prints of our pieces have started to connect with more people. The arts remain the thread, now woven into something truly our own.

Our house was once The Fairweather Shop, a local landmark for 27 years where Mary Fairweather sold antiques from the basement and a 'curiosity shop' in the barn next door. This property actually hosted the first business in town, a blacksmith and farrier's shop more than 200 years ago (we still dig up horseshoes in the yard!). For the last decade, that same barn served as home to Littleton Sign Company, run by Shannon and me.
